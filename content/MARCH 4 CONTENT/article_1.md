# OpenAI calls its own Pentagon deal 'sloppy'

OpenAI signed a deal with the Pentagon. Less than a week later, it's rewriting it.

After Anthropic was [blacklisted from federal contracts](https://www.anthropic.com/news/statement-comments-secretary-war) last week, OpenAI [stepped in](https://openai.com/index/our-agreement-with-the-department-of-war/) as the Department of War's replacement AI provider. The contract it signed contained none of the surveillance safeguards Anthropic had demanded. The backlash came fast.

Now CEO Sam Altman is [amending the deal](https://openai.com/index/our-agreement-with-the-department-of-war/) to explicitly ban the use of its AI for domestic mass surveillance, adding references to the Fourth Amendment, the National Security Act of 1947 and the FISA Act of 1978. None of those protections were in the original agreement.

Altman defended the Pentagon work internally but admitted to staff that the company "shouldn't have rushed" and called the deal "opportunistic and sloppy," according to the [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/openai-ceo-altman-defends-pentagon-work-to-staff-calls-backlash-really-painful-76d769ec). That kind of concession doesn't come easily from Altman.

The political pressure explains the speed. President Trump [called](https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/116144552969293195) Anthropic's leadership "Leftwing nut jobs" for refusing to loosen safety restrictions for military use, and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth followed through with an unprecedented supply chain risk designation. The implicit message to every other AI lab was hard to miss.

What looked like an open goal quickly became what [Forbes characterized](https://www.forbes.com/sites/the-prompt/2026/03/03/how-anthropics-kerfuffle-with-the-pentagon-became-a-optics-headache-for-openai/) as "an optics headache for OpenAI." By rushing in without the guardrails Anthropic was punished for insisting on, OpenAI ended up spotlighting exactly what its own contract was missing.

The pushback came from inside the labs, too. Ninety-six OpenAI employees and 629 Google staffers signed a [joint open letter](https://notdivided.org/) objecting to the pressure campaign.

"They're trying to divide each company with fear that the other will give in," the letter read. "That strategy only works if none of us know where the others stand."

Google employees are separately [pushing for explicit "red lines"](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/02/26/technology/google-deepmind-letter-pentagon.html) on military AI within their own company, according to The New York Times. This kind of cross-company employee coordination is new territory, and it's putting real constraints on what labs can agree to behind closed doors.

Users had their own response. ChatGPT saw a 295% surge in app uninstalls on Feb. 28, according to analytics firm Sensor Tower. Anthropic's Claude climbed to [No. 1 on Apple's US App Store](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/28/anthropics-claude-apple-apps.html), boosted by a #CancelChatGPT campaign that gained traction across social media.

"There are no winners in this," Lauren Kahn, a senior research analyst at Georgetown's Center for Security and Emerging Technology, [told CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/27/anthropic-pentagon-ai-policy-war-spying.html).

The ripple effects continue. Defense contractors including Lockheed Martin are [pulling Anthropic's AI from classified systems](https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/society-equity/defense-contractors-like-lockheed-seen-removing-anthropics-ai-after-trump-ban-2026-03-04/), according to Reuters, creating operational disruption across the defense supply chain. And OpenAI, far from cementing its position as the Pentagon's go-to AI partner, is patching a contract it should have gotten right the first time.

**Our Valley View**

The contract revision is the right call, but the speed of the walk-back is revealing. Consumer trust in AI companies is thinner than the industry likes to admit, and a 295% spike in uninstalls is the kind of number that should make every AI CEO think twice before chasing a government deal without doing the legal homework first. The more consequential development might be the employee letter. When workers at rival companies start coordinating on shared ethical red lines, it gets much harder for any single lab to quietly fold under political pressure. The Pentagon wanted to divide and conquer the AI industry. It may have accidentally united it.
