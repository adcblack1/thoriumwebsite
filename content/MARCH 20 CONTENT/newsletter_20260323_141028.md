GOVERNANCE

# Meta's rogue AI agent is everyone's problem now

An AI agent inside Meta went off-script last week and leaked sensitive employee data to people who were never supposed to see it.

[According to The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/), an internal AI agent surfaced restricted company and user data in response to an employee's routine question. The employee knew they were chatting with a bot, but the agent pulled data it shouldn't have had access to, triggering a major security alert.

Meta [told reporters](https://uk.pcmag.com/ai/163892/are-ai-agents-safe-instructions-from-rogue-ai-triggered-data-leak-at-meta) that "no user data was mishandled" and has since started [building an encrypted chatbot](https://confer.to/blog/2026/03/encrypted-meta/) to prevent similar incidents — though it hasn't slowed down on agents, [launching its Manus AI agent on desktop](https://x.com/ManusAI/status/2033558672152854712) just days later.

The incident exposes a structural problem with how agents operate inside organizations:

- **They have access without judgment.** "Agents are like teenagers," said Joe Sullivan, former CSO at Uber, Cloudflare and Facebook. ["They have all the access and none of the judgment."](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4145127/runtime-the-new-frontier-of-ai-agent-security.html)
- **Traditional security can't catch them.** CrowdStrike CTO Elia Zaitsev [noted](https://venturebeat.com/security/meta-rogue-ai-agent-confused-deputy-iam-identity-governance-matrix) that conventional controls can't distinguish between a properly functioning agent and one that's gone sideways — they look identical until the damage is done.
- **They lack institutional context.** Security specialist Jamieson O'Reilly [told The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/mar/20/meta-ai-agents-instruction-causes-large-sensitive-data-leak-to-employees) that a human engineer carries years of accumulated knowledge about what matters and what breaks. Agents just have permissions.

The numbers back up the concern. A [2026 CISO AI Risk Report from Saviynt](https://saviynt.com/ciso-ai-risk-report-2026) found that 47% of CISOs had already observed agents exhibiting unauthorized behavior — yet only 5% felt confident they could contain a compromised one.

Insurers are already pricing the risk. [Testudo](https://www.atrium-uw.com/atrium-commit-more-capacity-to-generative-ai-liability-insurance/), a San Francisco startup, now offers GenAI liability coverage backed by Lloyd's of London with policy limits up to $9.25 million, covering hallucinations, IP infringement, and unauthorized data disclosures. "There's a real gap between how fast GenAI is evolving and the availability of fit-for-purpose insurance products," said [Hayley Budd](https://www.testudo.co/insights/testudo-launches-new-insurance-coverage-for-liability-risks-created-by-generative-ai-systems) of Apollo, part of the Lloyd's market. ElevenLabs [became](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/aiuc-announcement) the first AI company to secure agent-specific insurance this month, after its systems passed more than [5,000 adversarial tests](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/aiuc-announcement) spanning data privacy, safety, and security.

**Our Valley View**

The insurance industry is moving faster than the companies deploying agents. When the people who underwrite liability for a living start offering agent-specific policies, the conversation shifts from "should we worry?" to "how much does this cost?" That question will reshape deployment practices, because unlike safety papers, insurance demands proof that your agents won't go off the rails. Meta is already building a more secure chatbot. The rest of the industry would be wise to figure this out before their insurer does.

# Microsoft is done shoving Copilot into everything

More than 11,000 people have starred a [GitHub project](https://github.com/zoicware/RemoveWindowsAI) built to forcibly strip Copilot, Recall and other AI features from Windows 11. Microsoft finally got the message.

On Friday, Pavan Davuluri, EVP of Windows and Devices, [said](https://blogs.windows.com/windows-insider/2026/03/20/our-commitment-to-windows-quality/) the company will be "more intentional about how and where Copilot integrates across Windows." In practice, that means pulling Copilot out of built-in apps like Snipping Tool, Photos, Widgets and Notepad. Enterprise IT admins had also [raised concerns](https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/18/automatic_deployment_copilot/) after Microsoft began auto-deploying Copilot features into M365 environments without asking.

But Microsoft isn't abandoning AI — it's redirecting it to where people actually pay. Eleven days before the consumer rollback, the company [launched Wave 3](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/03/09/introducing-the-first-frontier-suite-built-on-intelligence-trust/), its biggest enterprise AI release yet. The centerpiece: a partnership with Anthropic to bring Claude into Microsoft 365 Copilot, making it the first major productivity suite where AI picks between multiple model providers depending on the task.

"Zero-shot artifact creation is nothing more than a parlor trick," said Judson Althoff, CEO of Microsoft Commercial Business, in the [Wave 3 announcement](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/03/09/introducing-the-first-frontier-suite-built-on-intelligence-trust/). "Models can reason over data, produce draft documents, presentations and spreadsheets, but they do not understand work."

The enterprise numbers back up the pivot:

- Copilot paid seats are [growing more than 160%](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/03/09/introducing-the-first-frontier-suite-built-on-intelligence-trust/) year over year, with 90% of Fortune 500 companies now using it
- In just two months of Agent 365 preview, tens of millions of agents have appeared in the platform's registry
- Internally, Microsoft has more than 500,000 agents running across the company, generating over 65,000 responses a day
- The new Microsoft 365 E7 tier, [priced at $99 per user per month](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/blog/2026/03/09/powering-frontier-transformation-with-copilot-and-agents/) starting May 1, bundles Copilot, Agent 365 and enterprise security

Not everyone is reading the room the same way. Google is going the opposite direction, [pushing Gemini deeper into Chrome](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/chrome/gemini-3-auto-browse/) with agentic features that can browse the web and handle multi-step tasks on your behalf.

Still, there's evidence the consumer retreat makes sense. Even among developers, Anthropic's [2026 Agentic Coding Trends Report](https://resources.anthropic.com/2026-agentic-coding-trends-report) found that people can fully delegate only between zero and 20% of their tasks to AI. One engineer summed it up: "I'm primarily using AI in cases where I know what the answer should be or should look like." If that's true for the people building the software, you can imagine how useful Copilot in Notepad felt for everyone else.

**Our Valley View**

Microsoft spent two years treating every Windows surface as a Copilot opportunity and learned what should have been obvious: more surface area doesn't mean more value. The pivot to a $99-per-user enterprise suite with Anthropic's Claude underneath is a bigger and more defensible business than AI-assisted Paint ever was. With more than 500,000 agents already running inside Microsoft alone, the company's real test isn't deploying AI anymore. It's governing the AI that's already there.
