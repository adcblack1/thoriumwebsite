# Inside OpenAI's $110B round, investors are hedging

OpenAI just closed the largest private funding round in history. The fine print suggests its biggest backers aren't writing blank checks.

On Friday, OpenAI [announced](https://openai.com/index/scaling-ai-for-everyone/) $110 billion in new funding from SoftBank, Nvidia and Amazon, pushing the company's post-money valuation to roughly $840 billion. The round alone is [more than four times larger](https://www.businessinsider.com/openai-funding-round-4-times-bigger-than-largest-ipo-2026-2) than the biggest IPO ever recorded.

"We're pushing the frontier across infrastructure, research, and products to make AI more capable, reliable, and broadly useful," CEO Sam Altman [said](https://openai.com/index/scaling-ai-for-everyone/).

The headline number is staggering. The deal structure is more revealing.

Nvidia's final commitment of $30 billion [came in well below](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/the-100-billion-megadeal-between-openai-and-nvidia-is-on-ice-aa3025e3) the $100 billion letter of intent it originally floated — a 70% reduction. Amazon committed $50 billion, but significant portions are [tied to milestone triggers](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/AMZN/8-k-amazon-com-inc-reports-material-event-2fc225386aa1.html) including OpenAI either going public or achieving what the deal defines as AGI. The exact definitions of those milestones remain heavily redacted in public filings. SoftBank anchored the round with $30 billion.

Perhaps most telling: Microsoft, OpenAI's largest existing investor and closest partner, [sat this one out](https://www.wsj.com/tech/openai-raises-110-billion-a2a34d23).

For Amazon, this is as much a cloud play as a financial one. CEO Andy Jassy was direct about the strategic calculus: "Today, we now have the two largest AI labs who are both significantly betting on Trainium," he [told CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/27/amazon-open-ai-cloud-jassy-altman.html). Trainium is Amazon's custom AI chip, built to compete with Nvidia's GPUs. Getting OpenAI to run workloads on it would be a coup for AWS and a direct challenge to Nvidia's dominance in AI compute.

Investors are clearly bullish on AI's potential. They're less certain about the economics.

"Investors will want to see improving margins, stable usage trends and evidence that OpenAI can defend its position," Dimitri Zabelin, a PitchBook analyst, [told Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/openai-funding-round-4-times-bigger-than-largest-ipo-2026-2). "This round secures capital and computing power. The next test is proving that the economics of large-scale AI can support that valuation."

For perspective, Anthropic recently closed a $30 billion Series G at a [$380 billion valuation](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-30-billion-series-g-funding-380-billion-post-money-valuation). Together, the two largest AI labs have raised over $140 billion in a matter of weeks. Neither has shown that frontier AI can sustain these numbers over the long haul.

**Our Valley View**

$110 billion is a remarkable war chest. It's also a round full of hedges: Nvidia slashed its commitment by 70%, Amazon tied its check to milestones no one will define publicly, and Microsoft stayed home entirely. The Amazon angle deserves attention — by pulling OpenAI onto Trainium chips, Amazon isn't just writing a check, it's positioning itself to control the compute supply chain that AI labs depend on. The question for OpenAI is no longer whether it can raise capital. It's whether it can prove frontier AI is a sustainable business before the next round of investors shows up with even more strings attached.
