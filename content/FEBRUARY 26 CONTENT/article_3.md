GOVERNANCE

# Anthropic's Friday deadline: bend or break

The Pentagon just gave Anthropic an ultimatum. What happens next could reshape the relationship between AI companies and the federal government.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has given Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei until Friday to drop Claude's safety restrictions for military applications, [according to CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/24/anthropic-ai-hegseth-spying-defense.html). If Anthropic refuses, the Pentagon is weighing two escalation paths: invoking the Defense Production Act, a wartime-era law that gives the government power to compel companies to prioritize its orders, or designating Anthropic a "supply chain risk," which would effectively blacklist the company from working with government contractors.

One senior official [told Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/02/24/anthropic-pentagon-claude-hegseth-dario) that the agency is "going to make sure they pay a price for forcing our hand like this."

The Pentagon's emotional case isn't subtle. In a December phone call with Amodei, a senior defense official posed a scenario to the Anthropic CEO: "What if a nuclear-armed intercontinental ballistic missile were hurtling towards the US with only 90 seconds to spare, and Anthropic's AI were the only way to trigger a missile response to save the country?" [Bloomberg Law reported](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/big-take-anthropic-pentagon-feud-is-about-more-than-ai-limits).

It's the kind of question engineered to make any answer but "yes" sound negligent. The legal ground underneath it is far less certain.

Joel Dodge of the Vanderbilt Policy Accelerator noted that using the DPA to "compel a company to produce a product that it's deemed unsafe, or to dictate its terms of service" would be "without precedent under the history of the DPA." And there's judicial skepticism on the national security framing. In a [2021 case](https://ecf.dcd.uscourts.gov/cgi-bin/show_public_doc?2021cv0280-21) where the DoD designated Xiaomi a security risk, Judge Rudolph Contreras wrote that the court was "somewhat skeptical that weighty national security interests are actually implicated here."

What happened to Xiaomi after that designation is worth paying attention to. Its stock [dropped 10.26%](https://ecf.dcd.uscourts.gov/cgi-bin/show_public_doc?2021cv0280-21), roughly $10 billion in market cap vanished, banks suspended linked financial products and index providers cut the company from global benchmarks. Anthropic, valued at $380 billion with an IPO reportedly on the horizon, faces potentially steeper consequences. A supply chain risk label could rattle the enterprise customers that generate the bulk of its roughly $14 billion in annual revenue.

The financial math makes the Pentagon's position harder to square. The contract in dispute is worth about $200 million, or roughly 1.4% of Anthropic's revenue. The contract itself isn't the weapon. The designation is.

**What Anthropic already changed**

While the standoff grabbed headlines, something arguably more significant happened in parallel. Anthropic [published version 3.0 of its Responsible Scaling Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/news/responsible-scaling-policy-v3), rewriting the safety framework the company has built its entire identity around.

The RSP is essentially Anthropic's public pledge about what it will and won't do as its AI models grow more capable. The [previous version](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/24a47b00f10301cd/original/Anthropic-Responsible-Scaling-Policy-2024-10-15.pdf), published in October 2024, made a concrete commitment: if Claude's capabilities crossed certain danger thresholds and safety measures couldn't keep pace, Anthropic would pause development. No wiggle room.

Version 3.0 removes that hard pause. Instead, the company will weigh "the costs and benefits of different mitigation options." Anthropic was [unusually candid](https://www.anthropic.com/news/responsible-scaling-policy-v3) about why: "Some parts of this theory of change have played out as we hoped, but others have not."

Two things went wrong:

- Anthropic's leaders hoped rivals would match their hard pause commitment. While OpenAI and Google DeepMind adopted broadly similar frameworks, none went as far.
- They expected the RSP to serve as a blueprint for binding federal AI regulation. While some state and international laws incorporated similar principles, the comprehensive federal framework Anthropic envisioned didn't materialize.

Jared Kaplan, Anthropic's chief science officer, was blunt: keeping the company from training new models "while competitors raced ahead without safeguards would not help them keep up in the AI race."

The new RSP also contains a buried irony. Anthropic's own framework [acknowledges](https://www.anthropic.com/news/responsible-scaling-policy-v3) that its highest proposed security standard, the one designed to fend off top-tier nation-state cyberattacks, is "currently not possible" and "will likely require assistance from the national security community." The same national security community now threatening to designate Anthropic a pariah.

The timing of it all is hard to ignore. On the same day Anthropic met with Pentagon officials, it released the new RSP and launched a [major enterprise product expansion](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/anthropic-touts-new-ai-tools-weeks-after-legal-plug-in-spurred-market-rout-2026-02-24/). One company, simultaneously loosening its safety commitments, fighting the government and shipping new enterprise tools.

Chris Painter, director of policy at METR, a nonprofit focused on evaluating AI safety, raised concerns about a "frog-boiling" effect: danger gradually escalating without any single moment triggering the pause the original policy was built to enforce. On the other side, Dean Ball, a former Trump administration AI policy advisor at the Foundation for American Innovation, warned that invoking the DPA this way would tell American entrepreneurs that "if you disagree with us politically, we're going to try to put you out of business." When even voices within the political coalition pushing the invocation are raising red flags, the legal and philosophical terrain is clearly uncharted.

**OUR VALLEY VIEW**

The $200 million Pentagon contract is a rounding error on Anthropic's balance sheet. The supply chain risk designation is the real weapon, a financial kill switch that could spook the enterprise customers keeping the lights on. But the larger question isn't whether Anthropic folds on one military deal. It's that any AI company can now face government coercion over its safety decisions, with no legal framework to referee the dispute. Anthropic's RSP was supposed to be the industry's moral floor. The fact that Anthropic itself quietly rewrote it before the deadline even arrived tells you everything about who's winning.
